## Overview

Picture an investor deciding where to put their money. The usual routine is five different apps for prices, news and calculators, plus a stock tip in their inbox that leaves them second-guessing every decision.

Active traders have the same problem at a faster pace: data scattered across tools, insights that arrive late, and not enough clarity for decisions that carry real money.

**TradeRake is a stock-market intelligence platform built to fix that** — one place where investors get real-time market intelligence, traders get actionable insight, and everyone gets a clear, transparent view of the market.

I designed it at Baron Tech Labs: the brand and marketing website first, then the product behind it. The company closed abruptly, and the site is now reduced to a single branding page — so the screens in this case study come from screenshots I saved while it was up. The site also had About us and Contact us pages, but it went down before I could capture them, so they are not shown here.

## Home page

The home page had one job: explain what TradeRake is, prove it covers the whole market, and earn an early-access sign-up from a product that had not launched yet. Each section was built to carry the visitor one step further toward that ask.

[[3]]

**Hero –** A clear value proposition next to a live app preview — portfolio value and watchlist — so the product is shown working, not just described.

**Explore global markets –** Six asset-class cards — stocks, mutual funds, bonds, commodities, forex and precious metals — in a scannable grid that shows full market coverage at a glance.

**Essential tools –** Six calculators (SIP, margin, brokerage, CAGR, EMI, inflation) that solve a real annoyance: people juggling separate calculator apps for their financial planning.

**Blog –** Articles on trading psychology, market basics and strategy, positioning TradeRake as somewhere to learn, not only a data tool.

**About TradeRake –** Four trust pillars — NSE and BSE integration, global markets, live news and smart trade calls — that separate it from a generic tracker.

**A glimpse of the future –** A teaser dashboard that previews the in-app experience and builds anticipation for early access.

**Testimonials and FAQ –** Short reviews from investor and trader personas for social proof, then plain answers to what TradeRake is and where it stands — both placed right before the sign-up to remove the last hesitation.

**Get early access –** The page closes on a clear, low-commitment action that fits a pre-launch product.

## Markets page

Six asset classes behave very differently and attract different people, so each one gets its own row, with alternating text and mockup placement and a distinct accent colour. The rhythm keeps the page scannable; the colour tells the markets apart.

[[4]]

**Global equity markets –** Real-time analytics and stock screeners, with a portfolio-growth mockup that shows data depth for serious traders.

**Wealth creation (mutual funds) –** Long-term, goal-based investing and SIPs, backed by a fund-analysis preview and a rising SIP value chart.

**Secure your future (bonds) –** Framed as the stable option, with a lock visual and an AAA credit rating to build trust.

**Trade global resources (commodities) –** Positioned as an inflation hedge, with live gold and crude prices across MCX and NCDEX.

**Trade forex –** Major currency pairs and a live buy signal for active, technically driven traders.

**Timeless value (metals) –** Digital gold, silver and sovereign gold bonds as a low-hassle way to hold precious metals.

## Blogs and tools

Both libraries were designed to grow, so they share one pattern: category tabs and search to jump straight to what you need, and a consistent card that makes a long list easy to scan.

**Blogs –** Tabs for All, Stock, Wealth and Finance. Each card carries a category tag, date, title and "Read article". The mix — psychology, beginner basics, market fundamentals — supports readers at every stage of their investing journey.

[[5]]

**Tools –** Tabs for Investment, Trading and Personal Finance. Each card has a category tag, icon, name and a one-line description, so a wide range of calculators can be compared at a glance.

[[6]]

**CMS-driven –** Articles and calculators are both managed through a CMS, so the team could publish a new article or add a new tool without design or development support.

## What I learned

**Design for the stage the product is at –** A pre-launch site cannot sell features people cannot use yet. Showing the product working, then asking for a small commitment, did more than a long feature list would have.

**Structure is what makes dense data readable –** One repeated pattern with a distinct accent per market let six very different asset classes sit on one page without blurring together.

**Build for the team, not just the launch –** Making blogs and tools CMS-driven meant the team could keep the site growing without waiting on design or development.

**Projects can end without warning –** Phase 2 was 90% designed when the company closed, and the site went down with it. Saving screenshots and documenting as I went is the only reason this work can still be shown.
