  - type: heading
    text: Overview
  - type: text
    body: >-
      Picture a buyer at a growing company, tasked with sourcing laptops,
      desktops and accessories. The usual route is endless phone calls,
      scattered emails and bulky spreadsheets that make comparing slow and
      frustrating. Sellers have it no better: outdated systems, missed
      opportunities and a lack of transparency that takes the excitement out of
      competitive bidding.


      **CyberBid is a mobile eAuction app that puts the whole thing in one
      place.** Sellers create an auction in a few taps, buyers bid in real time,
      and both sides see the same clear picture of what is happening.


      It was my capstone project on a UI/UX bootcamp, and I owned the full cycle
      — from framing the problem space to validating a high-fidelity prototype
      with users.
  - type: heading
    text: Why online auctions
  - type: text
    body: >-
      Buying and selling at auction is fragmented, slow and inefficient. Sellers
      rely on outdated methods to reach buyers, while buyers work across
      scattered platforms with no real-time view of what they are bidding on. I
      wanted to reimagine that space as a mobile-first platform that makes the
      process faster, fairer and more intuitive for both sides.


      Two things became the spine of the project, and both came out of the
      research rather than my assumptions.


      **Transparency.** Buyers and sellers both need a fair, real-time view of
      auctions, bids and outcomes.


      **An end-to-end experience.** Instead of juggling emails, calls and
      separate tools, one place to post items, bid, track and award — less
      friction, more trust.
  - type: callout
    label: Problem statement
    body: Design a mobile-first eAuction platform from scratch that connects sellers
      and buyers in a transparent, fair and competitive marketplace. The app
      should streamline the process from auction posting to bidding and
      awarding, while making online auctions simple, engaging and trustworthy.
  - type: heading
    text: Who it is for
  - type: text
    body: >-
      **Sellers** — small businesses, retailers and individuals posting products
      or lots for auction.


      **Buyers** — professionals, businesses and individuals who want a quick,
      fair and engaging way to bid and purchase.


      Both groups deal in collectible electronics, vintage tech and digital
      goods, are aged 18–60, and live in Tier 1 and Tier 2 Indian cities. They
      run into the same four obstacles: authenticity, unclear auction timelines,
      shipping risk and restrictive platform policies.
  - type: heading
    text: Research
  - type: text
    body: >-
      **Primary research.** I ran surveys and interviews with buyers and sellers
      who use e-auction platforms across India, to find out what actually
      frustrates them, what they wish existed, and where the process breaks down
      day to day.


      **Secondary research.** Alongside that, I looked at where Indian auction
      platforms are heading: real-time bid tracking, automated alerts and
      simpler interfaces to reduce friction; a growing demand for verified
      listings to cut fraud; and bulk listing and analytics tools becoming
      essential for smaller sellers.


      The interviews confirmed most of my early hypotheses and reshaped the
      problem definition. Unverified listings, unclear bidding, missing seller
      reputation systems and poor shipping communication came up in almost every
      conversation.
  - type: heading
    text: Buyer pain points
  - type: text
    body: >-
      **Unverified listings.** Incomplete product descriptions make evaluation
      difficult, and there is a real risk of counterfeit or refurbished
      hardware.


      **Stressful bidding.** Last-minute bidding wars create anxiety, and
      unclear timelines mean missed opportunities.


      **Legitimacy concerns.** It is hard to verify that a rare console or
      collectible is what the listing claims.


      **Shipping issues.** Damaged packaging destroys collector value, and
      fragile items make delivery a gamble.

  - type: heading
    text: Seller pain points
  - type: text
    body: >-
      **Trust barriers.** Buyers question product condition and functionality,
      and are sceptical about the legitimacy of digital licences.


      **Value gaps.** Buyers often do not know what vintage tech is worth, so
      its true value is hard to convey during a sale.


      **Operational challenges.** Shipping delicate hardware is expensive, and
      payment disputes over software keys are common.


      **Platform restrictions.** Rules limiting digital sales reduce flexibility
      in how sellers list and distribute.

  - type: heading
    text: Where existing tools fall short
  - type: text
    body: >-
      **No personalisation.** Listings and recommendations are generic, ignoring
      a buyer's interests, budget or collecting history. Nothing learns from
      past bids or saved searches.


      **Fragmented tools.** Discovery, bidding, payment and shipping live in
      different apps and sites, which causes confusion and missed auctions.


      **Limited control.** Sellers get little flexibility in listing formats or
      auction types — timed, reserve, buy-now. Buyers get little control over
      alerts and watchlists.


      **Cluttered interfaces.** Information is inconsistent across platforms, so
      new or casual users struggle to follow a bidding flow or check that an
      item is genuine.


      **No end-to-end journey.** Most platforms cover listing or bidding. Few
      carry a user from discovery through verification and payment to secure
      delivery.
  - type: heading
    text: Competitor analysis
  - type: text
    body: >-
      **eBay India** — huge inventory across categories, a global reputation and
      established buyer protection. But the interface is complex, localisation
      for Indian users is thin, support is minimal and the bidding rules are
      unclear.


      **OLX Auctions** — popular for second-hand goods, easy for local
      transactions, broad user base. But it has no formal auction features such
      as timed bidding, and listings often go unverified, which erodes trust.


      **Saffronart** — specialist in art and antiques, with expert curation and
      authenticated listings. But it serves a niche audience and offers little
      in the way of bulk upload or wider seller support.


      **PowerBid India** — localised for industrial and commercial auctions,
      strong at asset auctions and auction management. But it barely touches
      consumer goods and lacks real-time updates and intuitive seller tools.
  - type: heading
    text: Hypotheses going in
  - type: text
    body: >+
      - **Verified listings and transparency** — clear descriptions, verified
      sellers and

      authenticity checks will cut buyer hesitation and build trust

      - **A streamlined bidding experience** — structured timelines, bid
      notifications and

      automated bidding will lower anxiety and reduce missed auctions

      - **Stronger trust mechanisms** — seller verification, escrow payments and

      authenticity guarantees will answer legitimacy concerns

      - **Reliable shipping** — secure packaging, insurance and live tracking
      will reassure

      both sides

      - **Value awareness tools** — market insights and benchmarks will help
      sellers show

      what an item is worth and help buyers judge it

      - **Flexible platform policies** — safer digital sales and broader
      categories will

      bring more sellers in

  - type: heading
    text: How might we
  - type: text
    body: >+
      Each pain point became a question to design against.


      **For buyers**


      - How might we make sure every listing is verified, to build trust and
      prevent fraud? - How might we simplify the bidding process so buyers can
      place and track bids in

      real time without confusion?

      - How might we validate sellers and products transparently, so buyers feel
      confident? - How might we give clear shipping information and a channel to
      the seller, so

      nothing is left uncertain?


      **For sellers**


      - How might we help sellers build credibility through reputation systems
      and verified

      profiles?

      - How might we give sellers analytics and promotional tools that make
      their auctions

      more visible and more successful?

      - How might we streamline listing, including bulk uploads, so managing
      many auctions

      is easy?

      - How might we offer flexible listing templates so sellers can represent
      products

      properly?

  - type: heading
    text: What I learned
  - type: text
    body: >-
      Research changed what I built. My instinct was that the bidding screen was
      the heart of the product; the interviews showed that trust decides whether
      a bid ever happens, so verification and seller reputation moved to the
      centre of the design.


      Designing for two audiences at once also meant every decision had two
      readings. A change that makes a listing faster to post can make it harder
      for a buyer to judge, and holding both sides in view was the hardest and
      most useful part of the project.
