  - type: heading
    text: Overview
  - type: text
    body: >
      Picture a buyer at a growing company, tasked with sourcing laptops, desktops
      and accessories. The usual route is endless phone calls, scattered emails and
      bulky spreadsheets that make comparing slow and frustrating. Sellers have it no
      better: outdated systems, missed opportunities and a lack of transparency that
      takes the excitement out of competitive bidding.


      **CyberBid is a mobile eAuction app that puts the whole thing in one place.**
      Sellers create an auction in a few taps, buyers bid in real time, and both sides
      see the same clear picture of what is happening.


      It was my capstone project on a UI/UX bootcamp, and I owned the full cycle —
      from framing the problem to validating a high-fidelity prototype.
  - type: heading
    text: The problem
  - type: text
    body: >
      Buying and selling at auction is fragmented and slow. Sellers use outdated
      methods to reach buyers; buyers hop between platforms with no real-time view of
      what they are bidding on. Existing apps are web-first, complicated for casual
      users, and cover only a slice of the journey — usually listing or bidding, never
      discovery through to delivery.


      I surveyed and interviewed buyers and sellers who use Indian auction platforms,
      and studied four competitors (eBay India, OLX Auctions, Saffronart and PowerBid
      India). The same frustrations came back again and again.

  - type: callout
    label: Problem statement
    body: >
      Design a mobile-first eAuction platform that connects sellers and buyers in a
      transparent, fair and competitive marketplace — covering posting, bidding and
      awarding, and making online auctions simple, engaging and trustworthy.
  - type: heading
    text: How I worked
  - type: steps
    items:
      - title: Research
        text: Surveys and interviews with buyers and sellers, plus a teardown of four
          Indian auction platforms.
      - title: Define
        text: Personas, journey maps and How Might We statements built from the pain
          points that came up most.
      - title: Structure
        text: User flows for registration, buyer and seller, then the information
          architecture for the whole app.
      - title: Design & test
        text: Low-fidelity wireframes sized for common Indian devices, then
          high-fidelity screens in Figma and a clickable prototype put in front of users.
  - type: heading
    text: What I designed for
  - type: text
    body: >
      Two decisions shaped everything else.


      **Transparency.** Both sides see the same live view of an auction: the current
      bid, the time left, and who the seller is. Verified listings and seller profiles
      were the direct answer to the trust problem that dominated my research.


      **One place, end to end.** Instead of juggling emails, calls and separate tools,
      the app carries a user from discovering an item, to bidding, to tracking, to the
      final award — so nothing gets lost between the steps.


      Around those, structured auction timelines and bid notifications take the anxiety
      out of bidding, and seller tools (bulk listing, analytics) fix the operational
      grind that sellers described.
  - type: heading
    text: The interface
  - type: text
    body: >
      I started with digital low-fidelity wireframes to iterate quickly and keep the
      layout honest against real screen sizes. Once the flows held up, I built the
      high-fidelity screens in Figma with the platform's colour, type and iconography,
      then wired them into a clickable prototype for usability testing.
  - type: heading
